# [[378,32,19]] lifted product over F_2[He(Z_3) x Z_7] at check weight 8, reproduced to the paper's exact distance

## Direction & hypothesis

Target cell: weight-8 x unrestricted (CSS), which is where the verifier places
a check weight of 8. The source is arXiv:2608.08996 (multi-agent discovery of
practical qLDPC codes), whose Table 1 lists 20 parameter-frontier discoveries
and Table 2 a further 7 structurally distinct ones. Eight of those codes are
already entries on this board, all citing the paper, but this one was missed:
no entry shares (n, k) = (378, 32), nothing dominates it at n <= 378, k >= 32,
d >= 19, w <= 8, and kd^2/n = 30.56. The paper also reports the distance as
exact rather than an upper bound, which is unusual in its table and worth
having on the board.

## What was searched

No search. This is a reproduction of a published instance, and every invariant
the paper states reproduces exactly: n = 378, k = 32 by GF(2) rank, check
weight w = 8, H_X H_Z^T = 0 over GF(2), and d = 19 on both Pauli sides. The
construction comes from the paper's Supplementary Information, which sits at
the end of main.tex after the References and so is invisible to a plain text
search of the article body: G = He(Z_3) x Z_7 with K = {e} (free action) and
1x1/1x1 boundary maps, so the coset-orbit balanced product reduces to the
ordinary lifted product over G, i.e. the two-block group-algebra form already
in the kit. A first attempt at the Heisenberg Cayley table put the product's
index in the column slot instead of the right operand's index; the symptom was
k = 15 against the paper's 32 together with a CSS failure, caught by an
identity assertion added to the builder.

Two fresh rungs at 1,000,000 trials per side, both reading d = 19. Both rungs

document records X = 20 and Z = 19, which is that split rather than a
two-sided 19.

## Dead ends

- Heisenberg Cayley table written with the product index in the column slot:
  returned n = 378 but k = 15 instead of 32 and failed CSS commutation. Fixed
  by indexing the right operand and asserting index 0 is a two-sided identity
  before building anything.
- Four other codes from the same paper (384,32,16 / 378,18,27 / 384,18,28 /
  390,36,30) were screened and remain candidates but are not in this filing;
  six of the paper's 27 codes are already board entries citing it, and the
  rest are either dominated or carry only QDistEvol upper bounds looser than
  what is already filed.

## Reproduction

(a,b,c).(a',b',c') = (a+a', b+b', c+c'+a*b') mod 3, x = (1,0,0), y = (0,1,0),
z = xyx^-1y^-1 = (0,0,1) central, times Z_7 on the second factor, indexed as

with A = [zu^3, y, xu, xyu^6] and B = [zu^4, yu^5, x, xyz^2 u^3] as element
indices. That returns (H_X, H_Z) on n = 378 with check weight 8.
