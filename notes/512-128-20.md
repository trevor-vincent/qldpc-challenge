# [[512,128,20]] — pair-partition code over F_4[C_32] with a full regular canonical frame

## Direction & hypothesis

Target cell: `weight-9plus` × `unrestricted` (CSS), which is where the verifier
places a check weight of 10 (`verify/qldpc_verify.py`, `weight-9plus`).
The opening was numerical rather than structural: arXiv:2610.06820 tabulates
twenty-four LP and PP codes whose distances it certifies exactly, and checking
each (n, k, d) against codes/ showed five were absent and of those only this
one was undominated. At kd2/n = 100.00 it sits well clear of the cell's
incumbents while encoding a quarter of its block length, which is the rate the
paper was built around: a full regular canonical logical frame of bounded
weight alongside the distance.

## What was searched

No search. This is a reproduction of a published instance: the cyclic group
C_32, the field F_4, three seed rows with eight exponent/coefficient pairs
each, all fixed by the catalogue entry. Rebuilt with the source authors' own
reconstruction routine (github.com/yeon-lee/public-regular-logical-frames,
src/rlf.py :: reconstruct), which reads the construction spec and emits the
binary check matrices. Verified here against the published values: n = 512, k
= 128, maximum row weight 10 on both CSS sides, and H_X H_Z^T = 0 over GF(2).
The paper reports this distance as exact: it states exhaustive full-code
searches exclude every logical operator lighter than a known witness. The
witness search run here is independent of that and is only an upper bound.

4 fresh-seed rung(s) agree at the best bound, the deepest of them at 1,000,000

Four fresh rungs on two independent seeds (20261012, 881122), each rung flat
at d = 20 on both Pauli sides, the deepest two at 1,000,000 trials per side.
The submitted witness reproduces it: witness-backed upper bound, confidence
upper_bound, not certified exact. The library gf2_fast backend ran the ladder
at 8 threads; the submission document carries its own separate search at
15,000 trials per side via surrogate.lightest_logical.

## Dead ends

- A hand-built expansion of the quaternary checks into binary columns gave n =
  1024 rather than 512 and failed CSS commutation: the pair-cancellation
  constraint of Eq. (19) and the reversing-symmetry placement of the
  correction pivots are not implied by the exponent arrays alone, so the
  source's reconstruct() is the construction rather than an implementation
  detail of it.
- Nine other codes from the same catalogue (lp-f4-476-56-14, lp-f4-544-64-15,
  pp-f4-768-192-13-w8 and others) were built and checked; each is either
  dominated on (n, k, d, w) by an existing entry or still awaiting its witness
  search.

## Reproduction

uv run python, then: catalogue =
json.load(open('catalogue.json'))['constructions']; code = next(x['code'] for
x in catalogue if x['code']['id'] == 'pp-f4-512-128-20'); hx, hz, seeds =
reconstruct(code). That gives the binary (H_X, H_Z) directly. The construction
itself is the pair-partition of arXiv:2610.06820 Sec. V: a reversing r-by-2M
PP array over R = F_4[C_P] with P = 32, r = 3, M = 4, seeds placed on disjoint
correction pivots P = (0,1,2) and P' = (4,5,6) with the shared free
coordinates J = (3,7). Exponents and coefficients are in the catalogue entry.
